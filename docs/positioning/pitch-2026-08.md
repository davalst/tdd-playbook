## The mission and the one-liner

**Mission (the founding reason, and the customer-facing promise):** AI-written code
that is provably equal to or better than the human baseline — fewer bugs, sound
architecture, features live in production rather than dark — and that keeps improving
even as smarter models get better at gaming the checks. Verification is the *mechanism*
and the moat; better code is the *product*. (The investor edition,
`pitch-2026-08-investor.md` v2, leads with this framing; this document remains the
mechanism-level diligence companion.)

**One-liner (mechanism level):** we make AI improvement claims provable. Signed,
replayable, offline-verifiable verdicts on agent work — and calibrated strength ratings
for the gates that check it.

**Go-to-market note (v2):** the playbook (guards, adversary agents, harness) is
open-sourced as the top of funnel; the calibration bank stays sealed (Decision 1);
community adopters contribute failure *shapes* — situation descriptions reproduced on
synthetic fixtures, never their source — through the plant-authoring pipeline
(shape → synthetic plant → contributor confirms → provenance-tracked approval). This
generalizes Phase 4 incident mining from one founder to a community and is the
structural answer to the escalation-ceiling kill criterion.

## The problem (30 seconds)

AI agents now write a material share of the world's code, and every vendor claims their
agents are *getting better*. Nobody can prove it — not to a customer, not to an auditor,
not to themselves:

- A CI badge says a check ran. It cannot prove the check *catches anything*, or that the
  agent didn't weaken it. The adversary that matters isn't an internet attacker; it's
  **the coding agent running with the developer's own privileges** — documented failure
  modes: weaken the gate, fake the green, tamper with the checker.
- Every test suite and eval harness is a **decaying asset** — models memorize public
  benchmarks (GSM8k had to be rebuilt as GSM1k to reveal 13% overfitting; SWE-bench is
  partially memorized), and nobody measures their own gate's recall or false-positive
  rate. Teams fly blind on the one instrument they trust.
- The peer-reviewed record says self-assessment doesn't work: LLMs cannot reliably
  self-correct reasoning without external feedback (ICLR 2024), and multi-agent debate
  frequently loses to cheaper baselines at matched compute (ICLR 2025) — yet the market
  is about to be flooded with exactly those unfalsifiable "self-improving" claims.

Procurement, regulators (EU AI Act conformity), and insurers underwriting agentic
development are all converging on the same question: **who verifies the verifier?**

## The insight

Recursive self-improvement has two halves. **Generation is a commodity** — every lab
sells it, and it compounds on their schedule, not yours. **Verification that stays valid
while the generator improves is the bottleneck** — it's what every RSI paper concludes,
and what aviation, clinical trials, and lab medicine each spent decades institutionalizing
(independent review boards, controls on every run, pre-registered criteria, item banks
treated as wasting assets).

We spent a year building that half, and we can prove it — because proving things is the
product. An independent analysis of recursive-improvement architectures, compiled without
reference to our stack, converged mechanism-for-mechanism on what we had already shipped
(convergence table in Appendix A). We didn't follow the literature; we collided with it.

## The product — three layers, sold as two

**1. The Verdict Layer (wedge) — memrebel + CIVerd.**
An open standard (`memproof-2`: canonical JSON, Ed25519 with domain separation, Merkle
inclusion, offline replay) plus a fail-closed verdict engine. A CIVerd verdict is a
signed, self-contained record of a CI run that anyone can re-verify offline against a
pinned key — *"was release SHA X actually verified — prove it, without trusting whoever
is telling me."* Absence of evidence is RED; the signing key never touches the process
running hostile tests; a red build cannot ship because `bundle_authentic` and
`verdict_ok` are separate facts. This is the Sigstore/SLSA playbook applied to agent
work: open spec, reference implementation, hosted service.

**2. Gate Certification (franchise) — the calibration service.**
"UL certification for CI gates." We maintain a private, continuously-refreshed bank of
**planted defects with paired clean controls**, run it against a customer's gates and
agents, and issue a **signed strength rating**: recall, false-positive rate, confidence
intervals, trend — replayable offline like any other verdict. The bank is a wasting
asset by design (published plants burn on a model-generation fuse as they enter training
data), which is precisely why this is a subscription: **corpus authoring is the standing
operating cost that becomes the recurring-revenue rationale and the moat.** The
actuarial industry has run this exact shape — private data, scheduled experience
studies, certified outputs — profitably for a century.

**3. The Improvement Engine (internal; never sold, always cited).**
The TDD Playbook: guards, adversary agents, and the calibration loop that measures them.
We don't sell it — labs will commoditize agent scaffolding. We run it on ourselves and
publish its **evidence stream**: the improvement ledger (every process change carries a
pre-registered expected effect, scored by the next calibration run), the append-only
scoreboard, the signed attestations. It is the demo that sells layers 1 and 2.

## Why now

- **Agent-authored PRs are flooding CI.** The volume of machine-written changes makes
  "did the gate actually run, and does it actually catch things" a board-level question,
  not a developer preference.
- **Compliance demand is arriving before supply.** EU AI Act conformity assessment,
  SOC2-style attestation for AI development, and insurance underwriting for agentic
  coding all need exactly one artifact that doesn't exist off the shelf: an
  independently verifiable record that the safeguards ran and that the safeguards work.
- **The claims race has started.** Every vendor will say "our agents improve." The
  first vendor whose claim comes with pre-registered predictions, measured deltas, and
  signed, replayable history wins the diligence — and sets the evidentiary bar the
  others get measured against.

## Why us — the moat, honestly stated

1. **The corpus and its history cannot be fast-followed.** A competitor can copy the
   spec (it's open — that's the point). They cannot copy a private, incident-mined,
   provenance-tracked plant bank with a dated scoreboard behind it, because the asset
   *is* the accumulated history. Real defect distributions are the one thing model
   self-play can't saturate — and every customer engagement mines more of them
   (data network effect).
2. **The integrity machinery is the product working on itself.** Append-only scoreboard
   enforced mechanically against the last release tag; oracles never weakened without a
   journaled entry; corpus immutable and grow-only; release tags created *only* by a
   script that verifies a fresh signed verdict for the exact SHA shipped — no bypass
   flag exists. A skeptic doesn't have to trust us; they can replay us.
3. **We are positioned as the DSMB, not the drug.** "Human-governed recursive process
   improvement with cryptographic audit" is what safety-conscious enterprise buyers and
   regulators want more of. It tells the exciting story *and* survives the diligence —
   the rare position in this market.

## Business model

- **Open spec, paid trust.** `memproof-2` and the reference verifier stay open (adoption
  is the distribution strategy — Sigstore's path). Revenue: hosted verdict
  service (issuance, key custody, storage, dashboards) and **Gate Certification
  subscriptions** — recurring because the item bank is a wasting asset that must be
  re-authored on a schedule, priced like the standing audit function it is.
- **Land:** one repo's release pipeline gated by signed verdicts (days to integrate; the
  verifier is stdlib-only). **Expand:** organization-wide attestation → gate
  certification → the improvement-ledger evidence stream as the org's own auditable
  "our process improves" claim.
- **Cost structure honesty:** corpus authoring and adjudication are the COGS. That is
  not a bug to engineer away; it is why the service is recurring and why the moat
  deepens with every customer incident mined into the private bank.

## Pre-registered kill criteria — stated before we're attached

The company applies its own discipline to itself. Written down now, dated, in the open:

1. **Lift ≤ noise at matched budget** → the improvement-engine *story* dies. The Verdict
   Layer and Gate Certification stand on their own — verification is valuable even if
   our process isn't — and the pitch changes to say so. (This experiment runs before
   any external claim; the v2.0 gate enforces it.)
2. **Escalation ceiling** — if frontier doers saturate everything adversary-tier
   authoring plus incident mining can produce, calibration-as-product dies. Early
   warning line: the per-plant vitality (saturation-share) trend, read quarterly.
3. **Platform attestation *with* calibration** — if GitHub ships measured gate strength,
   the standalone play compresses to the corpus and history. Response is already in
   motion: accumulate the data that can't be fast-followed, now.

## Appendix A — Technical credibility: where this sits in the RSI literature

**The taxonomy.** Order 0: an agent improves code (every vendor). Order 1: the system
improves the *process* that produces code — guards, prompts, gates (the Playbook:
incident → guard → planted test). Order 2: the system improves the *measurement of
improvement itself* — the calibration co-evolution loop, where an adversary authors new
planted defects, the corpus grows with provenance, and gate strength is itself gated.
Order 2 is the rare one, and it's where we live.

**The classical anchor.** Schmidhuber's Gödel machine (2003): self-modification is only
safe and only real when modifications are accepted with proof of improvement.
Impractical as stated — but our architecture is the practical approximation:
modifications to the verifier require out-of-loop human authorization, an append-only
audit trail, and cryptographic non-repudiation. **The one component an optimization loop
must never optimize is its own scorer, and scorer-modification is the single most
controlled operation in our system.** Verified consequence, not aspiration: the ledger's
first REFUTED entry (B-004) shows the instrument catching a wrong fix in one cycle.

**The structural placement.** Our doer/plant-author pair is a GAN with grounded rewards
— generator vs. adversary, fitness settled by mechanical verdicts. Same structural
reason AlphaGo's self-play and AlphaEvolve's evaluator-grounded search work, and the
same reason two-agent *conversational* loops fail their own literature (no ground
contact). We evaluated and explicitly rejected building the conversational loop; the
decision and its evidence are on the record
(`docs/evaluations/two-agent-recursive-loops-2026-08.md`,
`docs/plans/rsi-hardening-2026-08.md` §2).

**The convergence table** (independent analysis → what was already shipped here):
held-out sets with known answers → calibration plants with known verdicts · mutation-test
the scorer → planted-defect suites, "a verifier that cannot be made to fail is theater" ·
controls on every run → paired clean controls with a mechanical pair quota ·
pre-registered criteria and metric change logs → frozen oracles, append-only journals,
scoreboard integrity checks · an independent verifier whose independence doesn't depend
on itself → CIVerd · item bank as wasting asset → the decay principle, corpus
co-evolution, catalog refresh clocks · noise floor before claiming improvement → repeat
sampling with AMBER verdicts · verifier revalidation on capability change →
calibrate-before-trusting-new-doer policy.

**The honest limit, stated because stating limits is the brand:** this generalizes only
to domains with mechanical oracles. Code is where the bedrock is constructible —
consequence you can't reinterpret, feedback in seconds, held-out sets buildable from
hidden tests and exit codes. Strategy and judgment domains are unfalsifiable by
construction, and we do not claim them.
